Teams and projects

Create the teams, then the projects they run, and share out the roles.

A project is the working frame of a product. It is run by exactly one team of the organisation and identified by a key, for example PORTAIL. A team can run several projects.

Create a team

The owner and administrators open Projects in the top bar, then:

  1. choose New team, enter the Team name and choose Create the team;
  2. open the team in the Teams table, with Add members or the arrow: its panel opens on the right. Until it has a Scrum Master, the table shows “To appoint”;
  3. add people with Add to the team. Only members of the organisation join a team: a guest, from outside, never does;
  4. choose Make Scrum Master next to the person who will hold that role. The Scrum Master is part of the team.

Create a project

On the same page, choose New project, then enter in the window:

  • the Project name;
  • its Key, 1 to 10 capital letters and digits, including at least one letter. It is unique in the organisation and prefixes the project’s identifiers (PORTAIL-12);
  • its Product goal, required: the long-term aim readable by the business;
  • the Team that runs it.

A key that breaks these rules is refused. The person who creates the project becomes its first Product Owner.

Share out the project roles

Open a project from the list. Its page reads by tabs: Project roles, Writing stories, Practices, then, when relevant, Agent development and Webhooks. In Project roles, Product Owners and administrators add someone with Add a person, in a window, and choose their role:

RoleWhat the person does in the project
Product OwnerOwns the backlog and validates delivered stories.
StakeholderProposes, follows and comments on requests. A guest can only be a stakeholder, and only sees the projects shared with them.

A project always keeps at least one Product Owner.

A Product Owner can tick Can validate for a stakeholder: they become a delegated validator and will validate delivered stories, like the Product Owner, once validation arrives in Youm.

Write the stories

In the Writing stories tab, the Product Owner chooses the project’s story Format:

  • Simple, by default: what you want, and why;
  • User story: “As a…, I want…, so that…”.

With the User story format, they name the project’s Actors, for example Customer or Technician, then Save. In the Technical view, the team then chooses each story’s actor in the As a line; the story’s details then show “As a” the actor and “I want” the title, then the “Why” under “So that”. Choosing Defect in the request form offers an outline: what happens, what was expected, how to reproduce.

The Business view does not change: people still write simply what they want and why.

Set the practices

Only the team’s Scrum Master sets the project’s practices, in the Practices tab; others read a summary.

  • Estimation scale: Fibonacci, integers, or no estimation.
  • Sprint length: 5 to 30 days, 14 by default.
  • Automatic task assignment: never, when the task is created, or when it starts.
  • Limit of urgent tasks in progress: 1 to 20, or none if the field stays empty.

Export your data

Export the data downloads a ZIP archive: the donnees.json file gathers projects, stories, tasks, tests, comments and history, and the pieces-jointes folder holds the attached files.

  • In the Organization settings, opened from the Members page, the owner and the administrators export the whole organisation.
  • On a project’s page, they export that project, as its Product Owner does.

A missing file does not stop the export: it appears in donnees.json with an empty file.

Import from iceScrum

In iceScrum, download your project’s export, as XML or ZIP. On the Projects page, the owner or an administrator chooses Import from iceScrum, then this file. Nothing is saved at this step: Youm shows what it found.

  • People: each address is linked to the member who has the same one. A person with no member is flagged: invite them first, or leave No member. Their stories then come to you, and their comments keep their name.
  • States: the table shows how each iceScrum state becomes a Youm state.
  • Gaps: what will not be carried over identically. Attachments are not imported: keep your export.

Import the project creates a new project, in one go, in the chosen team. Story and task numbers, states, estimates, tests, tasks and comments are kept, and the velocity of finished sprints is recalculated from validated stories. Passwords found in the export are neither read nor kept.
