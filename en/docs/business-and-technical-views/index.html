Business and Technical views

Two ways to see the same requests, depending on your role.

Youm shows the same requests in two ways. The Business / Technical switch, at the top of the side menu, moves between them: the data stays the same, only the presentation changes. A guest does not see it and stays in the Business view.

The side menu serves both views. It lists the Projects, then the views of the current project; at the bottom, depending on your rights, Project settings, Teams and projects, Members, Subscription and Administration. The top bar keeps only the organisation, the bell and your avatar, whose menu holds My account, the language, Documentation and Sign out.

Business view: my requests

The Business view is for the people who express needs and validate what is delivered. It shows each request’s status in business words and the next step, without points or sprints. Each status has its colour, from the grey of a sent request to the green of a validated one.

Every request follows the same path: Request sent, Accepted, Planned, In progress, Awaiting validation, Validated.

When a version is under way, the home screen shows its due date and how many of its requests are validated.

Technical view: the backlog

The Technical view is for the Scrum team. A side menu, on the left, lists the Projects then the Views of the current project, each with its number of items: Proposals, Backlog, Planning, Task Board, Reports, Validated, All and Features. On a computer, the button at the top of the menu folds it into a column of icons, to give the whole width to the requests: each view’s name and number stay in its tooltip, and your choice is kept on your device. On a phone, the Menu button opens it as a drawer; it closes after a choice, with Escape or with a tap outside. The Technical view uses the full width of the screen. The list shows each story’s type, status and author, with type and status in colour. The Backlog view is described in the chapter “Estimate and rank the backlog”.

Filtering the Technical view

Above the stories of the Proposals, Validated, All and saved views, a Filter box gathers every filter. Typed words are searched in the stories’ key and title, ignoring accents and case. Typing a value’s name, such as “Defect” or a Feature’s name, suggests that filter; typing a criterion followed by a colon, such as “state:” or “état:”, suggests its values. The criteria are state, type, Feature (including “No Feature”), sprint, actor, person (including “me”) and “unestimated”. The arrow keys move through the suggestions, Enter adds one, Backspace removes the last filter, and the “/” key jumps to the box from the view.

Each filter added becomes a tag, removed by its cross. Two values of the same criterion add up (one Feature or the other), two criteria combine (a Feature and a sprint). The ranked backlog is never filtered: its full order is what reordering uses.

Saved backlogs

The Product Owner and the Scrum Master save the displayed view from All, with Save a view, in the header’s More actions menu (“…”): a panel asks for its name. The view keeps the displayed filter and layout (list, by Feature or board, with or without lanes). It appears in the menu for the whole team and follows the work, with nothing to keep up to date; opening it restores its filter and layout. When you change them, “View changed.” shows under the filter: Save updates the view for everyone (Product Owner and Scrum Master), Back to the saved view discards the changes. In a saved view, the More actions menu (“…”) offers Delete this view, after confirmation; it leaves the menu without touching the stories.

At the top of the lists, three cards break stories down By state, By type and By Feature (By author in Proposals); clicking a value adds it to the filter. The list sits in one card: the Filter box, the + Type, + State and + Person criteria, the order and the columns at the top; the number of stories and the applied sort at the bottom. In Proposals, Add a proposal creates a story from its title alone.

Team notes

In the Technical view, a story’s details include Team notes: implementation details, in Markdown, that the team and the Product Owner write with Edit. They never show in the Business view.

Decide on a proposal

The Product Owner opens a proposal from the Proposals view and chooses:

  • Accept: it enters the backlog;
  • Decline: they enter a reason, required, which the author will read;
  • Reopen, for a declined proposal: it becomes Suggested again.

Only the Product Owner takes these decisions. For others, these buttons do not appear.

List, by Feature or board

The List / By Feature / Board switch, above the requests, changes how they are shown. By Feature exists in the Technical view only. The Board puts the requests in columns, one per status, in the order of their path; each column shows how many requests it holds. An empty column folds into a narrow strip, its name reading top to bottom; the chevron in a column’s header folds or unfolds any column. In the Business view, the Accepted and Estimated states share the “Accepted” column. A card opens the request’s details: that is where a change of status is decided, never by moving the card. Your choice is kept on your device.

Each card shows its type on a tab, and the column holding it gives its status. On the team side, a bar has one segment per acceptance test: green if passed, grey if waived, red if failed.

In the Technical view, the board uses the full width. Each column header gives its number of stories and its total points, and stays visible while the column scrolls; each column scrolls on its own. A card shows its key, its Feature, the owners of its tasks (or else its author), its title on three lines at most, then the progress of its tasks, its tests and its estimate.

Clicking a card’s Feature, or initials, adds that filter to the Filter box: a person keeps the stories where they own a task or that they wrote. In the By Feature card, clicking a Feature’s name adds the same filter. Lanes by Feature splits the board into one band per Feature, each with its columns; the option is off by default and kept on your device. At the bottom of the Suggested column, Add a story creates a proposal from its title alone.

By Feature groups the stories under their Feature, in the Product Owner’s order, then those without one. A Feature’s row gives its number of stories, its total points and its progress: one mark per story in the colour of its status, solid once validated, then the number of validated stories out of the total and the percentage. A declined story stays in the list but does not count. Clicking a Feature’s row folds or unfolds it; Collapse all and Expand all act on every Feature. Under the Feature, each story shows its status, the owners of its tasks, its points and its tasks done out of the total.

Sort, choose columns and act on several stories

In the technical view, the List shows a Request column, then the columns chosen in Columns: type, state, Feature, actor, owners, points, tasks, author and sprint. Sort orders the list and the By Feature view by rank, key, title, state, points or Feature, in either direction; clicking the Request, State, Feature or Points header does the same. A missing value stays last. The ordered backlog always keeps the Product Owner’s order. A saved view keeps its sort and columns; “View modified” flags a change.

Clicking a row opens the story. Whoever is allowed to edits the Feature, the actor and the estimate in place by clicking the cell, and the title with a double click or the F2 key. The state is never changed from the list: it is decided in the story detail.

The box at the start of each row selects the story; Shift and click selects a run of rows, the header box all the rows shown. A bar then offers to attach the selection to a Feature, to plan it in a sprint (with a reason if the sprint is running), to accept the proposals or to follow the stories. Each story is judged by the usual rules: the summary gives the number of stories changed and, for each refusal, the key and its reason. Undo reverses what succeeded, when the action has an opposite.

With the keyboard, the arrows move from row to row, Enter opens the story, Space selects it and Escape clears the selection.
