Acceptance tests and validation

Check a story with its acceptance tests, move it to Awaiting validation, then validate it or request a correction.

A delivered story awaits the business’s decision. Its acceptance tests say what to check; validation is the final decision of the Product Owner or a delegated validator.

Define acceptance tests

In a story’s details, in the Technical view, the team and the Product Owner enter an Expected check then choose Add a test. Once the story has tests, New test opens the entry. Tests are numbered in order; a test’s arrows move it up or down one place, and the bin deletes it. A new test is To check. The optional Given / When / Then template button fills the entry with an outline to complete.

A test’s pencil opens Rephrase; the test goes back to To check. When the business has answered “How will you know it worked?”, each line of the answer arrives as a To check test marked To rephrase. Only the team sees this marker, which disappears once the test is rephrased. Such a test blocks validation like any other.

When the story is In progress or Awaiting validation, the team records each test’s result: Passed or Failed. A new result lifts any waiver, and the history keeps a trace of it.

Reword with the assistant

An organization can hand the rewording of stories to a writing assistant. It is turned off by default. An administrator turns it on in the Organization settings, opened from the Members page, Writing assistant section: they save the organization’s Anthropic API key, then tick Turn on the assistant for teams. The key is encrypted and never shown again; only its last four characters identify it, and Remove the key turns the assistant off. The audit log keeps each change, never the key.

Once the assistant is on, the team and the Product Owner find Reword with the assistant in a story’s details, technical view. The story’s title, why and tests are then sent to the organization’s AI service, which suggests a title and a why in the project’s format, Given / When / Then tests and remarks on the INVEST criteria. Nothing is saved until someone chooses Use this suggestion; Discard forgets it.

Once kept, the suggestion goes through the usual actions: tests To rephrase are rewritten in order, the others are added, and the actor is chosen. The author of a request still pending sees it rewritten; otherwise the rewording is added to the team’s notes, and the business request keeps its own words. The business never sees the assistant.

Grant a waiver

A failed or unchecked test blocks validation. Only the Product Owner can exempt it, while the story is In progress or Awaiting validation: Waive, on that specific test, with a mandatory Reason for the waiver. The test becomes Waived, distinct from Passed, and the waiver goes into the story’s history. A delegated validator cannot waive.

Move the story to Awaiting validation

When all the story’s tasks are done, a team member chooses Move to Awaiting validation. This is refused while a task is still open. This step always belongs to a human on the team: an agent never moves a story forward. While the story is awaiting validation, its tasks no longer move and it stays in its sprint.

Validate or request a correction

The Product Owner and delegated validators see Your decision under each request in the Awaiting validation block of the Business view home. Without opening the request, they can:

  • Open the staging environment, the link to the staging environment;
  • Validate, if each test is passed or waived; the request becomes Validated;
  • Request a correction, with a mandatory comment, then Send: the request goes back to In progress, its tests keep their results, and the comment stays in its history.

The first decision is enough: once the request is validated or sent back, another validator gets a refusal. A technical story, absent from the Business view, is decided from its details in the Technical view.

Validate first shows the test summary, with the reason for each waiver. Confirm validation records the decision, Cancel goes back without changing anything.

The request’s details also show its tests and the same decision.

The staging link

In the Planning view, the Product Owner enters the Project staging link from the “…” menu next to New release and, if needed, the Release staging link, which replaces it for that release’s stories, in Release settings, from each release’s “…” menu.

The version report

The version report brings together, for one version, what was requested, checked and accepted, and by whom. It is the document to hand to a client or an auditor: it speaks the language of requests, without sprints or points.

The Product Owner, delegated validators and administrators open it with Version report, in a Version’s details on the business side, or next to the release name in the Planning view. Others do not see this link.

The report can be drawn up at any time and shows its date, the name of the person who drew it up and the state of the version, finished or in progress. It lists the version’s requests, grouped by state (Validated, Awaiting validation, In progress, Planned), then by theme. For each: its why, its acceptance tests with their result and the reason for each waiver, and every decision taken, including correction requests. Technical stories are not included.

For a validated request, the report shows what was accepted at the time of validation, even if the request has changed since. If the actor or the theme changed after validation, a line says so: “Changed since validation: actor”. When a validation is cancelled and given again, the latest validation is the one that counts.

Print opens the browser’s print dialog, which can also save the report as a PDF. Download the CSV gives one row per request, with the number of tests per result, the validation and the number of correction requests, and says whether the request changed since its validation; this download is recorded in the audit log.
